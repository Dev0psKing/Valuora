# Product Requirements Document (PRD)

## Executive Summary

The proposed platform is a digital business valuation and intelligence platform designed to help small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) understand what their businesses are worth, why they are worth that amount, and what actions they can take to increase their business value over time.

Many SME owners operate successful businesses for years without having a clear understanding of their company's financial value. They may know their annual revenue or profit but cannot confidently answer fundamental questions such as:

* What is my business worth today?
* How would an investor value my business?
* How much should I sell my business for?
* Is my asking price realistic?
* What factors are reducing my company's value?
* How can I increase the value of my business?
* How does my business compare with others in my industry?

Professional business valuation services are often expensive, inaccessible, time-consuming, and primarily targeted at large corporations. Most small and medium-sized businesses cannot afford to regularly engage professional valuation firms.

This platform aims to democratize access to business valuation intelligence.

Using financial data, operational data, industry benchmarks, risk assessment, and multiple recognized valuation methodologies, the platform will generate an estimated business valuation range and provide users with detailed insights into the factors influencing their valuation.

The platform will not simply function as a calculator. It will help SME owners:

1. Understand their business value.
2. Monitor changes in business value over time.
3. Identify financial and operational risks.
4. Understand the factors driving valuation.
5. Simulate different business scenarios.
6. Discover actions that could potentially increase business value.
7. Generate professional valuation reports.
8. Compare their performance against relevant industry benchmarks.

The initial market focus will be small and medium-sized businesses, with particular attention to emerging markets and African SMEs, where access to structured business valuation tools remains limited.

---

# 1. THE PROBLEM

## 1.1 SMEs Often Do Not Know What Their Businesses Are Worth

A significant number of small and medium-sized business owners know their:

* Daily sales.
* Monthly revenue.
* Operating expenses.
* Profit.
* Number of employees.

However, they do not know the actual estimated market value of their businesses.

Business owners frequently estimate their business value using arbitrary assumptions such as:

> "I invested ₦20 million, so my business must be worth ₦20 million."

or:

> "My business makes ₦5 million profit every year, so it should be worth ₦100 million."

These assumptions are often not based on recognized valuation methodologies.

Business value is influenced by many variables, including:

* Revenue.
* Profitability.
* Cash flow.
* Growth rate.
* Industry.
* Business risk.
* Customer concentration.
* Assets.
* Liabilities.
* Debt.
* Market conditions.
* Recurring revenue.
* Owner dependency.
* Operational maturity.
* Competitive position.

Most SME owners do not have access to tools that combine these factors into a structured valuation assessment.

---

## 1.2 Professional Valuation Services Are Expensive

Professional business valuation can require the involvement of:

* Chartered accountants.
* Financial analysts.
* Investment bankers.
* Valuation specialists.
* Corporate finance advisors.

For many SMEs, professional valuation services may be too expensive to perform regularly.

As a result, valuation is often only conducted when:

* The business is being sold.
* An investor is involved.
* The company is raising capital.
* A legal dispute occurs.
* A merger or acquisition is being considered.

This means business owners often operate without understanding one of their most important financial metrics: the value of the business they are building.

---

## 1.3 Existing Tools Are Often Designed for Developed Markets

Many existing valuation platforms and methodologies are primarily built around:

* United States businesses.
* European businesses.
* Public markets.
* Venture-backed technology companies.

These models may not accurately reflect the realities of:

* African SMEs.
* Informal or semi-formal businesses.
* Emerging markets.
* High-inflation economies.
* Businesses with inconsistent financial records.
* Family-owned businesses.
* Owner-operated companies.

An SME operating in Nigeria, Kenya, Ghana, South Africa, or other emerging markets may face significantly different economic and operational conditions compared with a business operating in Silicon Valley or London.

The platform aims to eventually develop valuation intelligence that considers regional and industry-specific realities.

---

## 1.4 Business Owners Do Not Understand What Drives Value

Even when an SME owner receives a valuation from a professional, they may only receive a number.

For example:

> Estimated business value: ₦80 million.

However, the owner may not understand:

* Why the business is worth ₦80 million.
* What factors reduced the valuation.
* What factors increased the valuation.
* How the valuation could change.
* What operational improvements could increase business value.

The platform aims to make valuation understandable.

Instead of only saying:

> Your business is worth ₦80 million.

The platform should explain:

> Your business value is primarily driven by strong revenue growth and recurring customers. However, high owner dependency and customer concentration are reducing your valuation multiple.

---

## 1.5 SMEs Lack Business Value Improvement Intelligence

Most businesses focus on increasing:

* Revenue.
* Profit.
* Customers.

However, not every revenue increase automatically increases business value proportionally.

A business can generate high revenue but still have a relatively low valuation because of:

* Low margins.
* High owner dependency.
* Poor documentation.
* Customer concentration.
* Unpredictable revenue.
* Operational inefficiency.
* High debt.
* Weak management systems.

The proposed platform will help businesses understand the relationship between operational performance and business value.

# 2. THE SOLUTION

The proposed solution is a digital platform that uses financial modelling, valuation methodologies, industry data, risk analysis, and business intelligence to estimate the value of small and medium-sized businesses.

The platform will collect structured business information and process the data through a valuation engine.

The system will then generate:

* Estimated business valuation range.
* Estimated enterprise value.
* Estimated equity value.
* Business health score.
* Financial performance analysis.
* Risk assessment.
* Valuation confidence score.
* Valuation methodology breakdown.
* Key value drivers.
* Key risk factors.
* Business value improvement recommendations.

The platform will provide transparency into how the valuation was calculated.

# 10. CORE VALUATION METHODOLOGIES

The system should not rely on a single methodology.

The valuation engine will use multiple approaches depending on the type and maturity of the business.

---

## Method 1: Revenue Multiple Method

Formula:

Enterprise Value = Revenue x Industry Revenue Multiple.

Suitable for:

* High-growth businesses.
* SaaS businesses.
* Businesses where revenue is a strong indicator of value.

---

## Method 2: EBITDA Multiple Method

Formula:

Enterprise Value = Adjusted EBITDA x Industry Multiple.

Suitable for:

* Established profitable SMEs.
* Service businesses.
* Manufacturing companies.
* Logistics businesses.

---

## Method 3: Discounted Cash Flow

The system projects future free cash flow and discounts it to present value.

Suitable for businesses with:

* Reliable financial history.
* Predictable cash flow.
* Stable operations.

---

## Method 4: Asset-Based Valuation

Formula:

Adjusted Asset Value = Fair Market Value of Assets - Liabilities.

Suitable for:

* Manufacturing.
* Logistics.
* Agriculture.
* Asset-heavy businesses.

# 17. RISKS AND CHALLENGES

The platform must acknowledge several major challenges.

## Valuation Accuracy

Valuation is not an exact science.

The platform should provide:

* Valuation ranges.
* Confidence scores.
* Methodology explanations.
* Assumptions.

The platform should avoid claiming that its valuation represents an official or guaranteed market value.

---

## Data Quality

Many SMEs may have:

* Incomplete records.
* Inaccurate financial statements.
* Informal accounting.

The platform must include strong validation and confidence scoring.

---

## Industry Data Availability

Reliable private-company transaction data can be difficult to obtain.

The comparable database will take time to develop.

---

## Regulatory Considerations

The platform must clearly define whether it provides:

* Financial education.
* Business intelligence.
* Valuation estimates.

Professional valuation certification and regulated financial advisory activities may have additional legal requirements depending on jurisdiction.
